Starting point: 48 citations that do not exist

On 10 February 2026 the criminal chamber of the High Court of Justice of the Canary Islands fined a lawyer 420 euros. In an appeal submission he had cited 48 authorities, judgments of Spain’s Supreme Court and a report by the country’s judicial governing council, which do not exist. The court records that a general AI tool was used rather than one built for the law, and that the cross-check against the official case law database never happened. It sets the size of the fine at half the approximate annual cost of a subscription to an AI tool built for legal work. The ruling also went to the bar association at which the lawyer is registered.1

It did not stay a single case. On 7 July 2026 the labour chamber of the High Court of Justice of Galicia imposed 1,800 euros on a lawyer whose submission carried 24 citations that were invented, distorted or unrelated to the case; there too the court took its bearings from the annual cost of a tested legal tool.3 Cases of this kind are known from several European countries, and the duty behind them is older than the tools. As early as 9 September 2024 the First Chamber of Spain’s Constitutional Court formally reprimanded a lawyer for quoting verbatim from 19 decisions of that court, not one of which existed.4 For the judiciary itself, the Consejo General del Poder Judicial, the self-governing body of the Spanish courts, issued an instruction in January 2026 that requires real, conscious and effective human control over every use of such systems.5

From the other direction comes a recommendation that leaves many businesses at a loss. On 27 January 2026 the Spanish data protection authority published ten recommendations on handling AI tools. For work it advises sticking to your own organisation’s information and security rules and entering nothing that would give away confidential data about the company, its staff or its customers.6 It names no alternative. Anyone who follows that advice and still wants to work with their own documents needs a place where those documents are allowed to sit.

Now to the official surveys, because they refute a convenient thesis. According to the Banco de España business survey of around 6,300 companies, 19.9% use an AI system, 60% of them only on a trial basis and a mere 6% intensively. The obstacle in first place is a shortage of skilled staff at 45.8%, then excessive cost at 40.8% and the availability of a company’s own data at 37.8%. Regulatory hurdles including data protection were on the list of options as well, and landed behind those three.2 For the first quarter of 2025 the national statistics institute counts 21.1% AI use among companies with ten or more employees and 13.4% among the smaller ones.7 Anyone telling the story that regulation is holding Spain back is writing past these figures. The bottleneck is knowledge, and knowledge grows where an answer can be checked.

Who is affected: five groups that need a source reference

The question of where a sentence comes from is rarely a matter of taste. Behind it there is usually someone who has to answer for that sentence: before a court, before a regulator, before customers or before their own management. That is why the source reference is not an extra but the part of the answer that makes it usable at all.

Spain’s business structure makes the subject broader than it first sounds. As at 1 January 2025 the national statistics institute counted 3,310,824 active companies. 54.4% of them had not a single employee, and 95.1% stayed below ten.8 At that size there is no IT department to assess a tool, approve it and monitor it. There is one person finishing something off in the evening.

That data protection is no side note here shows in the authority’s annual figures: 30,931 complaints in 2025, up 64% on the year before, and fines totalling 48,108,765 euros.9 These figures cover data protection as a whole and not artificial intelligence in particular; we do not present them as AI statistics. They describe the setting into which a tool is introduced.

What connects the five groups below is not the sector but the kind of responsibility. At the end stands a statement that someone puts their name to. For statements like that, an answer without a source reference is not half as good, it is unusable.

Use case 1: the knowledge base in which every statement shows where it stands

Your company puts its documents into a knowledge base: contracts, instructions, minutes, handbooks, the correspondence on a matter. Someone asks which notice period applies to a particular type of contract. What comes back is not a smooth paragraph but an answer with the place it comes from behind it: document, section, passage of text. One click opens that passage in its context.

The difference lies not in the wording but in the checkability. An answer without an origin has to be believed. An answer with an origin can be checked, and checked at the place itself. That is exactly the step missing in the court cases described above, and exactly the step that turns a tool for drafting text into a tool for work.

The limit here: a knowledge base is only as good as its contents. If an outdated version sits in it, that version gets quoted with a source reference and looks more credible for it than before. Whoever puts documents in has to settle which version applies and who maintains it. That is work, and it cannot be handed over to a model.

Use case 2: an outside document against your own documents

A draft contract, an audit report or the new version of a rulebook comes in. The question is the same every time: what in it departs from what applies here? You set the outside document beside your own knowledge base. What comes back is a list of divergences: covered, open, contradictory, and for every line the place on both sides, in the outside text and in your own.

This task shows why the source reference has to sit on both sides. A divergence only becomes a divergence once someone has read the two formulations side by side. If one side is missing, what remains is an assertion nobody can retrace, and the check starts again from the beginning.

The limit here: the result is a pointer and not a decision. A language model can miss a divergence or invent one that is not there, and the longer the texts get, the more readily that happens. In regulated areas the list replaces neither the qualified professional nor the internal sign-off. This article is no substitute for legal advice.

Use case 3: the draft that stays recognisable as a draft

At some point the check turns into a text: a statement of position, an answer to a customer, an internal note. The AI colleague puts up a draft, and visibly as a draft. Every statement taken from your documents carries its source with it, and the source can be unfolded. Anything formulated without a basis in your documents is marked as such.

The gain lies in the order of reading. Someone handed a finished text reads it for style. Someone handed a draft with sources to unfold reads it for sources. That second posture is the one missing in the court cases. The design of a tool can encourage it, it cannot compel it.

The limit here: a draft does not become correct by showing sources. What counts is that someone opens them. Where deadlines press, that is exactly what gets skipped, and no tool can prevent it. Anyone introducing this workflow should plan the check in as a step of the work and not as a good intention.

Limits: what a source reference does not do

The most important sentence in this article is the most uncomfortable one. Source references do not prevent invented answers. They make them checkable. A language model produces text that sounds plausible, and it does so even when the basis is missing. Human control remains mandatory, and that is exactly what the courts say: the High Court of Justice of the Canary Islands speaks of a breach of the fundamental duty of human oversight, which it calls indispensable whenever AI tools are used, and requires such systems to be treated as an aid and never as the deciding authority.1 Anyone who promises you something else at this point is selling you a certainty that does not exist.

On top of that comes an error harder to spot than a freely invented source. A language model can also attach a source reference to the wrong place: the document exists, the section exists, only it does not carry the statement. In the Galician proceedings part of the criticised citations were of exactly that kind, formally present and unrelated to the case in substance.3 From that follows a plain rule: the source has to be opened, not merely present. A source reference that nobody clicks on is decoration.

On the state of the law, without turning it into advice. In Spain a bill on the governance of artificial intelligence has been tabled, published in the official gazette of the Congress of Deputies on 12 June 2026; it is in parliamentary deliberation and is not law in force.10 The European directive on network and information security, NIS2 for short, is not transposed into Spanish national law; on 8 July 2026 the European Commission therefore took Spain, together with three other member states, to the Court of Justice of the European Union.11 We therefore claim neither that your company meets one of these requirements with our tool, nor that it would already have to meet them today. Check that for your own case, and if in doubt with your trade association. This article is no substitute for legal advice.

And now the awkward point, the one about money. The state programmes with which small Spanish businesses financed their digitalisation are taking no new applications. The large digitalisation programme closed its final two calls on 31 October 2025, by its own account with around 850,000 supported businesses and more than 3.4 billion euros.12 The programme for subsidised digital consulting took its last applications back in March 2025 and has been in settlement since.13 Whoever starts now pays for it themselves. That argues for a low entry price and for free self-hosting under the MIT licence. Only self-hosting presupposes IT competence, and by the official figures that is precisely what these businesses lack most.2 We do not resolve this contradiction. For a business without its own IT, managed operation is the more honest route, even though it costs money.

Finally, what we do not claim. EU Cowork AI does not compile an inventory of all the AI systems in your company, it sees only what runs through itself. It does not create human oversight, it makes it demonstrable. Managed operation meets no requirement that calls for customer-held keys without provider access; only self-hosting can do that. And the Green data centres in Lupfig are certified to ISO 27001, which is that operator’s certification and not ours.

Frequently asked questions about source references, knowledge bases and proof

Does a knowledge base with source references prevent invented judgments?

No. It makes it possible to spot an invented item in seconds, because next to every statement stands the place it is supposed to come from. Whether anyone opens that place is decided by the person at the screen. That is precisely the step the Spanish courts called for in the cases that became known, and precisely where the failure lay. A tool can make the check easy, it cannot take it off your hands.

Are we allowed to put client files into an AI tool at all?

The Spanish data protection authority expressly advises against entering confidential information about the company, its staff or its customers into AI tools. It draws no distinction by form of operation. It names no alternative. With a managed or self-hosted workplace the difference lies in where the files sit and who is allowed to reach them; whether that is enough for your case depends on your contracts and your professional duties. This article is no substitute for legal advice.

Is there already a dedicated AI law in force in Spain?

No. There is a bill, published in the official gazette of the Congress of Deputies on 12 June 2026 and currently in parliamentary deliberation. Until it is passed it is not law in force, and its wording can still change. Offers that already derive duties or fines from it today are worth examining on exactly this point.

What does NIS2 mean for us in Spain?

The directive is not transposed into Spanish national law. The European Commission therefore took Spain to the Court of Justice of the European Union on 8 July 2026, together with three other member states. What follows from that for your company depends on the sector and can only be answered conclusively once the national law exists. We claim neither compliance nor non-compliance on this point.

Is there still state funding for digitalisation?

Not under the known programmes. The large digitalisation programme for small businesses closed its final two calls on 31 October 2025, and the programme for subsidised digital consulting took its last applications in March 2025. Whoever starts today funds it themselves. That is why the entry price is the decisive figure for small businesses, and why the software is available under an MIT licence for self-hosting.

We have no IT department. Can we still run this?

Self-hosting costs nothing, but it presupposes that someone sets up a server, updates it and backs it up. Where that competence is missing, managed operation is the more honest route, even though it costs money. We say so openly, because a shortage of skilled staff is the largest obstacle in the official surveys and a free offer does not solve that problem, it shifts it. What self-hosting means day to day is set out under Self-hosting.

Does our data train the models?

No. Customer data is not used to train models, and telemetry is switched off by default. Your documents, knowledge bases, conversation histories and accounts sit in the Green data centres in Lupfig or on your own server. Only the passage a single request needs goes to the model. The commitment stands in the data processing agreement, the parties involved in the subprocessor list.

What does managed operation cost?

It costs 15 € per person and month, in Switzerland 14 CHF, of which 8 € or 7.50 CHF respectively is model credit in your organisation’s shared pool. One access route opens more than 100 models, processed on the EU side, plus the Anthropic models through Amazon Bedrock in the European profile. The administration settles which models are on offer and which one is the server default. All the details are on the pricing page.
